Conservation Laws in Orbital Mechanics

Energy and angular momentum: two scalar quantities that never change as a planet sweeps around its ellipse.

Key equations

E = \tfrac{1}{2} \mu v^2 - \frac{GM\mu}{r}

L = \mu \, |\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v}| = \mu r v_\perp

E = -\frac{GM\mu}{2a}, \qquad L^2 = GM\mu^2 \, a(1-e^2)

Interactive demo

What you're seeing

The three horizontal bars are computed live from the planet's current position and velocity — nothing is precomputed or faked. Kinetic energy (blue bar) peaks at perihelion where the planet moves fastest, while potential energy (purple bar, extending left because PE is negative) reaches its most negative value at the same point. Their sum, the total energy E (yellow bar), stays flat throughout the orbit — that is energy conservation.

The angular momentum readout L = \mu r v_\perp in the top-left of the canvas also holds constant. This is not a coincidence: Kepler's 2nd law (equal areas in equal times) is exactly the statement L = \text{const} in disguise — when r is small, v_\perp must be large to keep their product fixed, which is why the planet moves faster near the Sun.

Try this

  • Pause near perihelion (rightmost point): KE is at its max, PE is at its most negative minimum, and the yellow E bar sits exactly where it always does.
  • Drag e to 0: a circular orbit. Because r never changes, both KE and PE are now individually constant — the bars freeze.
  • Drag a (semi-major axis): watch the total energy shift to a new constant level (E = -GM\mu/2a), and L shift too (L^2 \propto a).
