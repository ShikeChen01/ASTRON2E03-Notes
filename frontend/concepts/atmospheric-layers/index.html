Thermal Structure & Atmospheric Layers

Planetary atmospheres are divided into distinct layers by the sign of dT/dz — gravity, radiation, and chemistry each dominate in different altitude regimes.

Key equations

Hydrostatic equilibrium \frac{dP}{dz} = -\rho g

Dry adiabatic lapse rate (troposphere) \frac{dT}{dz} = -\frac{g}{c_p} \approx -9.8\,\text{K\,km}^{-1}\;\text{(Earth dry)},\quad -6.5\,\text{K\,km}^{-1}\;\text{(moist)}

Effective emission level (radiative equilibrium) \tau \approx \tfrac{2}{3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_{\rm eff} = T(\tau = 2/3)

Variables
P
atmospheric pressure (Pa or bar)
\rho
atmospheric mass density (kg m-3)
g
surface gravity (m s-2)
T
temperature (K)
z
altitude above the surface (km)
c_p
specific heat at constant pressure (J kg-1 K-1)
\tau
optical depth; photons escape freely when \tau \lesssim 2/3

What you're seeing

The plot shows the canonical temperature–altitude (or temperature–pressure for Jupiter) profile for the selected body. The blue troposphere is convectively controlled and cools with altitude following approximately the moist or dry adiabat. The orange stratosphere warms with altitude because ozone (Earth/Venus/Titan) or hydrocarbon aerosols (giant planets) absorb UV and near-UV radiation. The purple mesosphere cools again as the absorbing species become too thin. The red thermosphere rises sharply due to direct absorption of solar UV and X-rays by O, O2, and N2.

Dashed horizontal lines mark the tropopause, stratopause, and mesopause — the turning points where dT/dz changes sign. On giant planets there is no ozone, so the stratosphere is heated instead by CH4 photolysis and aerosol absorption; the tropopause sits near ~0.1 bar.

The readout box (top-left of the canvas) shows the surface (or 1 bar) temperature, tropopause temperature and altitude, and the peak thermosphere temperature where applicable.

Try this

  • Switch to Jupiter: the y-axis becomes log-pressure and there is no mesosphere — above the tropopause the temperature climbs monotonically due to hydrocarbon and aerosol UV absorption.
  • Compare Venus and Earth: Venus has a much hotter surface (735 K vs 288 K) but a similar tropopause temperature (~175 K vs ~217 K) because its dense CO2 atmosphere radiates efficiently at altitude.
  • Notice that Mars has no separate stratospheric warming layer — its thin atmosphere has negligible UV absorption, so the profile just has a troposphere and a gentle thermosphere.
  • On Titan, the tropopause is near 40 km at ~70 K, but the stratosphere warms to ~170 K at 200 km due to its thick haze absorbing UV.
