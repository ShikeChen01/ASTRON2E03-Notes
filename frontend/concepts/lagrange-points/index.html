Restricted Three-Body Problem & Lagrange Points

In the rotating frame of two massive bodies, five equilibrium points emerge — three unstable collinear points and two (potentially) stable triangular points.

Key equations

\mu_2 = \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}

L_4,\, L_5: \text{ equilateral triangles with } M_1,\, M_2

L_1 \approx R\!\left(1 - \left(\tfrac{\mu_2}{3}\right)^{1/3}\right) \quad L_2 \approx R\!\left(1 + \left(\tfrac{\mu_2}{3}\right)^{1/3}\right)

\text{Stability of } L_4, L_5{:}\ \mu_2 < \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 - \sqrt{\tfrac{23}{27}}\right) \approx 0.0385

What you're seeing

The view is in the corotating frame — a reference frame spinning with the two big bodies (M₁ yellow, M₂ blue) so they appear stationary. In this frame, gravity plus the centrifugal force produce an effective potential with five saddle points and minima: the Lagrange points L1–L5.

L1, L2, L3 (red markers) are collinear with the two masses. All three are unstable saddle points — a spacecraft placed exactly there will drift away unless it fires thrusters to station-keep. JWST orbits the Sun–Earth L2 point (just outside Earth's orbit) using small periodic corrections.

L4 and L5 form equilateral triangles with M₁ and M₂. They are stable (green) when the mass ratio is sufficiently lopsided: \mu_2 < 0.0385 (Routh's criterion). Jupiter's Trojan asteroids have been trapped at Sun–Jupiter L4 and L5 for billions of years. When \mu_2 exceeds 0.0385 the triangular points become unstable too — watch the color change as you drag the slider past that threshold.

Try this

  • Click Sun–Earth: L4 and L5 are green (stable). L2 sits just outside Earth's orbit — JWST lives there with active station-keeping.
  • Click Pluto–Charon: \mu_2 \approx 0.108 > 0.0385, so L4 and L5 flip to red (unstable).

  • Try Sun–Jupiter: the Trojan swarms at L4/L5 have over a million members — the mass ratio is well within the stable regime.
