The Greenhouse Effect & Radiative Transfer

How IR-absorbing gases warm a planet's surface — visualised with the N-layer slab model.

Key equations

T_s = T_\text{eq}\,(N+1)^{1/4}

\tau = \int \kappa_\nu \,\rho \,dz

T_\text{eq} = T_\star\sqrt{\frac{R_\star}{2a}}\,(1-A_B)^{1/4}

Variables
T_s
planet surface temperature (K)
T_\text{eq}
bare-rock equilibrium temperature with no atmosphere (K)
N
number of perfectly IR-opaque atmospheric layers
\tau
optical depth — dimensionless measure of IR opacity along a path
\kappa_\nu
mass absorption coefficient at frequency \nu (m² kg−1)
\rho
atmospheric mass density (kg m−3)
z
altitude (m)
T_\star
stellar effective surface temperature (K)
R_\star
stellar radius (m)
a
orbital semi-major axis (m or AU)
A_B
Bond albedo (fraction of incident light reflected, 0–1)

Interactive demo

What you're seeing

The diagram shows a cross-section of the atmosphere from the ground (bottom) to space (top). Each coloured slab is a perfectly IR-opaque atmospheric layer; darker slabs are cooler, brighter slabs are warmer. Orange/yellow arrows are thermal IR fluxes (\sigma T^4). Every layer absorbs all IR from below and re-emits equally upward and downward — this trapping is what raises T_s.

The yellow arrow from the sun symbol penetrates all layers to the surface, showing that visible light passes through greenhouse gases unimpeded. The right panel shows the resulting surface temperature and compares it to Earth's observed mean (288 K) and Venus's observed surface temperature (735 K).

Earth's T_\text{eq} \approx 255\,\text{K}; with roughly one effective greenhouse layer the model gives \approx 303\,\text{K}, close to the observed 288 K. Doubling CO2 contributes roughly 3.7 W m−2 of radiative forcing, equivalent to about 1–2 K of additional warming.

Try this

  • Increase N from 0 to 4 and watch the surface temperature climb as (N+1)^{1/4}.
  • Set N = 0. The surface temperature equals T_\text{eq} — no greenhouse warming at all.
  • Lower Teq to 232 K (Venus's bare-rock value) and crank N up — see how many layers it takes to reach Venus's actual 735 K surface temperature.
  • Note the "greenhouse excess" T_s - T_\text{eq}: it grows faster as N increases because each extra layer adds proportionally less, yet the absolute excess keeps rising.
  • Set Teq = 255 K and N = 1 — this is the simplest single-slab model of Earth, giving \approx 303 K.
